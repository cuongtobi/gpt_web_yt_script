## Selected hook audit

- Selected hook factual premise: Revenge can be delayed rather than impulsive; Herodotus explicitly connects Harpagus's later political actions to vengeance against Astyages, while Byzantine sources record Justinian II's 695 overthrow/mutilation, 705 restoration, and execution of Leontios and Tiberios.
- Evidence/source:
  - Herodotus, *Histories* 1.119–125 and 1.129: https://www.perseus.tufts.edu/hopper/text?doc=Perseus%3Atext%3A1999.01.0126%3Abook%3D1&force=y
  - Prosopography of the Byzantine Empire, Justinian II / Leontios / Tiberios: https://pbe.kcl.ac.uk/data/D41/F35.htm ; https://pbe.kcl.ac.uk/data/D51/F13.htm ; https://pbe.kcl.ac.uk/data/D82/F74.htm
  - *English Historical Review* on later source traditions for Justinian's restored reign: https://academic.oup.com/ehr/article/136/580/465/6337261
- Action: KEEP with existing attribution/qualification.
- Final wording: Preserve H4 mechanism. Keep "In Herodotus's telling" for Harpagus and describe Justinian's documented executions without claiming every policy of his second reign was motivated by revenge.

## Required changes

1. **Harpagus / Herodotus:** Remove the line implying that the word "legend" itself settles the status of the account. Keep the stronger plain-language distinction: Herodotus preserves an ancient story tradition, not a modern court record.
2. **Justinian II:** Replace "the Prosopography of the Byzantine Empire" in narration with a plainer description such as "a scholarly database that brings together the Byzantine sources." Keep the institution only if useful.
3. **Byzantine mutilation:** Phrase as a practice that *could* be used to disqualify defeated rivals; do not imply a universal legal rule.
4. **Caesar:** Replace "probable historical core" with "core story preserved by more than one later biographer." The surviving texts support a shared tradition, not a quantified probability.
5. **Boudica:** Replace "thousands of other people recognize their own grievance" with "many other people" to avoid an unnecessary numerical implication.
6. **Tomyris:** The wine-trap episode is in Herodotus and can be sourced, but it is not necessary for the central argument. Remove it from Final for tighter factual scope.
7. **Otrar:** Keep 1218, roughly 450 merchants, 1219 invasion, and the governor's capture/execution. Remove the molten-metal execution detail from Final because it is a later source-specific flourish and adds gore without analytical value.
8. **Otrar causality:** Continue saying the crisis helped precipitate the invasion while explicitly preserving strategic/imperial motives; do not reduce the Mongol campaign to personal anger.
9. **Audience language:** Replace academic phrases such as "forensic account," "source tradition," and institutional labels when a short spoken alternative works.
10. **Final timing:** Final must stay within 4,408–5,072 words for the 4,740-word ±7% gate.

All required changes have an explicit Final wording/action below; no VERIFY item remains.
