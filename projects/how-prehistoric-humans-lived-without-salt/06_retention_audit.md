## First 3 minutes verdict: PASS

- Central contradiction arrives immediately.
- Macro question is explicit.
- Real payoff appears before methodology: visible salt is not equivalent to sodium intake.
- Kidney mechanism gives a second payoff before the opening becomes background-heavy.
- Physical visualization is available from the first shot through nephron sequence.
- No CTA or literature-review opening.

## Act progression verdict: PASS

### Act 1
- Entry: impossible-looking survival without salt.
- Payoff: body conserves sodium; salt shaker not required.
- Consequence: need natural input sources.

### Act 2
- Entry: seek one ancestral source/menu.
- Payoff: ecology varies; Yanomami demonstrates low-added-salt physiology.
- Consequence: if low exposure is survivable, why produce salt?

### Act 3
- Entry: absence of early saltworks appears to imply no use.
- Payoff: preservation bias + Poiana positive evidence.
- Consequence: organized production needs a system-level explanation.

### Act 4
- Entry: maybe people suddenly “needed salt”.
- Payoff: food system changed; livestock/preservation/logistics expand demand.
- Consequence: salt becomes separable infrastructure/commodity.

### Act 5
- Entry: history seems like solving scarcity.
- Payoff: modern abundance reverses the original problem.
- Consequence: callback closes macro loop.

No `FLAT ACT`.

## Scale escalation verdict: PASS

Sequence repeatedly changes scale rather than staying in abstract physiology or archaeology:

`object → individual → ecology/community → individual/community → archaeological system → place/community → civilization/institution → global/system → individual callback`.

No 3+ consecutive beats at the same abstract scale.

## Scene density verdict: PASS

Playable or physical sequence density is strong:

- missing salt shaker / stripped meal;
- nephron sodium reabsorption + hormones;
- ecological map of sodium sources;
- 24-hour urine measurement;
- preservation comparison: stone/pottery vs soluble salt;
- Poiana salt spring / ash layers / brine heating;
- settlement + grain + herd + preservation logistics;
- ancient salt-processing chain → modern food system → kidney.

No >2-minute abstraction block if edited to outline.

## Reveal ladder verdict: PASS

- R1: no salt shaker ≠ no sodium.
- R2: kidney/endocrine conservation.
- R3: no universal hunter-gatherer diet; Yanomami low urinary sodium; archaeological silence is weak evidence.
- R4: salt industry emerges from a changing demand system rather than a newly evolved sodium requirement.
- R5: humanity externalized an old homeostasis problem into technology/economy, then created an abundance environment.

R4 changes the viewer model and is evidence-backed.

## Repetition/template flags

- Excess English code-switching: `salt shaker`, `landscape`, `game`, `spring`, `case`, `population`, `intake`, `supplement`, `demand`, `production`, `exchange`, `resource`, `scarcity`. Some technical English is acceptable, but current density weakens natural Vietnamese voice.
- Repeated “Điểm quan trọng...” construction appears more than once.
- The sequence “Có thể được sản xuất / dự trữ / vận chuyển / phân phối” feels rhetorically manufactured.
- Several paragraphs use “Đây là...” as opening/turn.

## Weakest-60-seconds test

Weakest stretch: approximately preservation-bias section before Poiana.

Why viewer still watches: they have just learned that low sodium physiology can work, so they now need the answer to a genuine contradiction—**why did people start investing labor in salt production at all?** The preservation paragraph must function as a short obstacle before the first major archaeological evidence, not a methods lecture.

Because that motivation is specific and the next payoff is concrete, the weakest stretch still passes after compression.
