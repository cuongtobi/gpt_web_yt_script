## Beat dependency test

1→2: Because visible salt was removed but sodium is still essential, viewer needs the body mechanism.
2→3: Because conservation cannot create sodium, viewer needs input sources.
3→4: Because sodium exists in food, viewer needs to know whether one ancestral menu can explain it.
4→5: Because ecology varies, viewer needs a real low-added-salt human case rather than a universal model.
5→6: Because low intake can work, viewer asks why archaeology shows salt production at all—and what missing evidence means.
6→7: Because archaeological silence is weak, viewer needs a site with positive evidence of organized production.
7→8: Because production is costly, viewer needs the system-level reason it became worthwhile.
8→9: Because agriculture changes dietary demand, viewer needs to see the extra demands that scale salt into infrastructure.
9→10: Because societies solved salt logistics, viewer naturally asks what happens when supply becomes abundant.
10→11: Because modern reversal is clear, central question can be answered and reframed.
