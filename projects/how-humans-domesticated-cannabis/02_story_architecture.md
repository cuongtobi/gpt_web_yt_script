# Story Architecture

## Viewer promise

The viewer will understand that Cannabis domestication was not one moment or one purpose: humans repeatedly selected a versatile plant for different traits, gradually producing fiber-, seed/oil-, and drug-oriented forms while movement and hybridization kept remixing them.

## Opening engine

**Opening image:** the charred interior of a 2,500-year-old wooden brazier from Jirzankal Cemetery in the Pamirs, with hot stones and chemically detected cannabinoid residues.

**Why it works:** the object is vivid and directly verified, but it is deliberately presented as a late chapter, not the beginning. The viewer is then pulled backward thousands of years.

**Surprising contrast:** the strongest direct evidence for psychoactive burning is thousands of years younger than the genomic model for initial domestication.

## Central question

How did humans transform one multipurpose Cannabis population into the very different crop forms we now call hemp and drug-type cannabis?

## Before-state

Before modern categories, Cannabis was a wind-pollinated, usually dioecious plant with multiple useful parts: edible/storable seeds, bast fibers in stems, and resinous flowers containing cannabinoids. Early human interaction could range from gathering to cultivation to selective propagation; these stages do not leave the same evidence.

## Causal ladder

### Step 1 — A versatile wild/feral plant attracts repeated human use
- Cause/condition: Cannabis offers several useful tissues and compounds.
- Change/problem produced: different communities can value different traits.
- Human/system response: gathering, movement, cultivation, seed saving.
- Consequence: human preferences begin filtering which plants reproduce.
- Support: C04, C09, C13.

### Step 2 — Repeated cultivation creates a domesticated gene pool
- Cause/condition: continued propagation and movement under human management.
- Change/problem produced: populations begin diverging from unmanaged ancestors.
- Human/system response: retain useful plants and seeds.
- Consequence: genomic signal of early East Asian domestication emerges.
- Support: C01, C02.
- Uncertainty: precise location/date remain model-dependent; C03, C08.

### Step 3 — Multipurpose cultivation persists
- Cause/condition: no single trait initially dominates all utility.
- Change/problem produced: general-purpose plants remain a compromise among seed, fiber and resin uses.
- Human/system response: maintain broad-use crops.
- Consequence: early domesticated Cannabis does not yet map neatly onto modern hemp/drug categories.
- Support: C04.

### Step 4 — Narrower economic/ritual goals increase selection pressure
- Cause/condition: communities increasingly reward particular outputs.
- Change/problem produced: a general-purpose plant is less efficient than a specialized one.
- Human/system response: repeatedly propagate plants with desired stem, seed, flower and reproductive traits.
- Consequence: divergence toward specialized crop packages around ~4,000 BP.
- Support: C05, C09.

### Step 5 — Selection reshapes plant architecture
- Cause/condition: fiber rewards long stems; flower/resin production rewards different branching and reproductive management.
- Change/problem produced: crop ideotypes diverge.
- Human/system response: select tall/unbranched fiber plants versus more-branched flowering drug types.
- Consequence: genomic sweep signals appear in branching, flowering and cell-wall pathways.
- Support: C06.

### Step 6 — Selection also reshapes chemistry
- Cause/condition: growers repeatedly favor chemical outcomes.
- Change/problem produced: cannabinoid pathway variants have different reproductive value under human selection.
- Human/system response: propagate preferred chemotypes.
- Consequence: THCAS/CBDAS loss-of-function patterns become associated with drug/hemp lineages.
- Support: C07.

### Step 7 — Human movement remixes the branches
- Cause/condition: crops move along exchange and migration networks.
- Change/problem produced: previously differentiated populations meet.
- Human/system response: intentional or accidental crossing and regional breeding.
- Consequence: modern genomes become mosaic rather than a clean bifurcating tree.
- Support: C08, C11.

### Step 8 — Archaeology reveals specialization in progress
- Cause/condition: intermediate populations leave seeds and other remains.
- Change/problem produced: archaeological categories overlap rather than appearing fully formed.
- Human/system response: local cultivation for mixed or changing purposes.
- Consequence: Haimenkou seed morphometrics show an overlapping population consistent with ongoing diversification.
- Support: C10.

### Step 9 — Explicit psychoactive ritual use becomes directly visible
- Cause/condition: some populations have high psychoactive potential and are deliberately burned.
- Change/problem produced: chemical residues preserve direct evidence of use.
- Human/system response: Jirzankal mortuary burning around 500 BCE.
- Consequence: proves psychoactive use by that date but does not prove it caused initial domestication.
- Support: C12.

1. Jirzankal sensory hook — novelty 4 / stakes 2
2. Evidence problem: use vs. domestication — novelty 3 / stakes 3
3. Genomic East Asia / ~12k model — novelty 4 / stakes 4
4. Reframe: early multipurpose crop — novelty 4 / stakes 4
5. Fiber vs. drug anatomy — novelty 4 / stakes 4
6. Reproductive control and chemistry — novelty 5 / stakes 4
7. Archaeological intermediate + Eurasian remix — novelty 5 / stakes 4
8. Return to Jirzankal — novelty 4 / stakes 5

Overall escalation: evidence becomes progressively more explanatory—from an object, to population history, to biological mechanism, to a revised model of domestication.

## Macro open loop

**Hook question:** If people were burning potent Cannabis 2,500 years ago, how much earlier did humans begin changing the plant—and what were they originally selecting it for?

**Payoff:** Humans likely began domesticating a multipurpose Cannabis population much earlier; modern hemp/drug specialization is a later branching process, and direct psychoactive evidence cannot be projected backward as the original motive.

## Micro loops and payoffs

1. **“Is 12,000 years ago a fact or a model?”** → payoff in genomic uncertainty section.
2. **“What does selection physically do to a plant?”** → payoff in architecture/cell-wall section.
3. **“How can farmers change chemistry without knowing chemistry?”** → payoff in synthase-gene section.
4. **“Why are hemp and drug categories not clean genetic boxes?”** → payoff with pangenome/hybridization.
5. **“Can archaeology catch a crop mid-transition?”** → payoff at Haimenkou.
6. **“Was getting high the original reason?”** → payoff when Jirzankal returns late in chronology.

## Major payoffs

- The ~12,000-year date is a genomic estimate that needs archaeological qualification.
- Early Cannabis was likely multipurpose rather than “hemp” or “marijuana.”
- Domestication selected anatomy, reproduction and chemistry together.
- Jirzankal proves potent psychoactive use by ~500 BCE but is not evidence for the original domestication motive.

## Uncertainty map

Use qualification for:
- “~12,000 years ago”: **model estimates / roughly / early Holocene**
- exact center: **genetic evidence points toward East Asia; precise geography remains unresolved**
- wild ancestor: **may still be represented by unsampled Asian relatives; feral/wild distinction is difficult**
- ~4,000 BP divergence: **model-based approximate timing**
- Haimenkou function: **consistent with / suggests**, not “proves”
- Jirzankal selection: high psychoactive potential is verified chemically, but whether plants were cultivated specifically for potency is **not established**
- multiregional use: does **not** equal multiple domestications

## Ending

**Answer:** humans domesticated Cannabis through repeated selection of a multipurpose plant, then intensified different traits for fiber, seed/oil and resin/psychoactive uses while moving and hybridizing populations across Eurasia.

**Larger implication:** domestication is not a switch from wild to domestic; it is a long evolutionary negotiation between human preferences and plant reproduction.

**Callback:** the tall stalk, stored seed and smoke from the Pamir brazier are revealed as different branches of the same long experiment.
