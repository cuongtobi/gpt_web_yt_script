# Research

## Central question candidates

1. How did repeated human cultivation turn an ancestral cannabis population into the very different fiber, seed/oil, and psychoactive forms we recognize today?
2. Was cannabis domesticated for intoxication, or did psychoactive cannabis emerge later from a much older multipurpose crop?

## Timeline / causal background

- **Early Holocene / early Neolithic:** A 2021 whole-genome resequencing study modeled the divergence of early domesticated ancestors from a basal cannabis gene pool at roughly 12,000 years before present. This is a genomic estimate, not a directly excavated domestication event, and the reported 95% interval is broad (6,458-15,728 years BP).
- **Early-to-mid Holocene:** Archaeological evidence indicates long-standing cannabis use in East Asia, but many early finds cannot by themselves prove full domestication or a specific use. Some seeds may reflect gathering, cultivation, or early crop management.
- **By the mid-to-late Neolithic:** Cannabis appears increasingly embedded in farming systems in parts of China. A 2026 phytolith study from Shandong reports cannabis in domestic contexts from roughly 4,500-3,400 BP, often alongside staple crops.
- **Roughly 4,000 years BP:** The 2021 genomic model proposes that a limited subset of earlier domesticated cannabis diversified into lineages that became increasingly specialized as hemp-type and drug-type cannabis. Archaeobotanical work broadly supports later specialization but cautions that the exact date is less secure than a clean genomic timeline can imply.
- **Bronze Age onward:** Fiber/oilseed forms and drug-oriented forms became more differentiated. Seed morphology, plant architecture, and genomic signatures all point to divergent human selection for different traits.
- **First millennium BCE:** Direct chemical evidence from Jirzankal Cemetery in the Pamirs shows cannabis with elevated psychoactive compounds being burned in wooden braziers around 500 BCE, demonstrating deliberate ritual use of high-THC material by at least 2,500 years ago.
- **Historic period:** Cannabis moved widely across Eurasia and later globally as fiber hemp, seed/oil crop, medicine, and drug. Human selection continued to reshape plant height, branching, flowering, fiber composition, seed traits, and cannabinoid chemistry.
- **Modern genomics:** Newer pangenome work published in 2025 finds more structural diversity and a more complicated hybridization history than earlier single-reference models captured, and concludes that wild relatives in Asia probably still exist and that the species gene pool remains incompletely characterized.

## Important uncertainties

1. **The ~12,000 BP date is not an archaeological timestamp.** It is a genomic demographic estimate with wide error bars and should never be narrated as an exact moment when a first farmer domesticated cannabis.
2. **The wild ancestor remains unresolved.** Many plants called "wild" may actually be feral descendants of cultivation; newer pangenome work suggests genuine wild relatives in Asia may still exist.
3. **Early use is hard to identify.** A seed or pollen grain does not automatically tell us whether people wanted food, oil, fiber, medicine, intoxication, or several uses at once.
4. **Modern hemp/drug categories can mislead when projected backward.** Early landraces were likely variable and multipurpose; sharp specialization developed over time.
5. **Direct evidence for psychoactive use is later than evidence for cultivation.** That supports, but does not prove, the idea that intoxication was not the original domestication driver.
6. **Genomic trees are not literal migration maps.** Gene flow, feralization, hybridization, and later breeding blur simple branching histories.
7. **Textile claims require care.** Some very early "hemp textile" identifications were based on impressions or older identifications that later scholars have questioned.
