# Research Ledger

## Provisional central question

How did repeated human choices transform Cannabis from an early multipurpose plant into increasingly specialized fiber, seed/oil, and drug-oriented crops?

## Causal steps to prove

1. Cannabis domestication was a process rather than a single event, and evidence of use is not automatically evidence of domestication.
2. Genomic evidence points toward an early East Asian domestication history, probably beginning in the early Holocene.
3. The early cultivated population was likely multipurpose rather than already divided into modern hemp and drug types.
4. Stronger divergent selection later favored different plant architectures and cell-wall traits for fiber versus flower/resin production.
5. Selection also altered cannabinoid synthase gene patterns.
6. Archaeobotanical evidence captures intermediate forms and shows that specialization was gradual and regionally complex.
7. Human movement and hybridization spread and remixed cannabis populations across Eurasia.
8. Direct chemical evidence for strongly psychoactive ritual use appears much later than the proposed beginning of domestication.
9. The exact wild ancestor, geography, and early chronology remain uncertain because Asian germplasm and ancient genomes are incomplete.

## Claims

### C01 — Genomic study supports early East Asian domestication
- Status: SUPPORTED
- Claim: A 2021 whole-genome resequencing study of 110 Cannabis accessions concluded that C. sativa was first domesticated in early Neolithic East Asia and that modern hemp and drug cultivars descend from an ancestral gene pool represented today by feral plants and landraces in China.
- Source: Ren, G. et al., “Large-scale whole-genome resequencing unravels the domestication history of Cannabis sativa,” Science Advances 7(29), eabg2286.
- URL/identifier: https://doi.org/10.1126/sciadv.abg2286 ; PMID 34272249 ; PMCID PMC8284894
- Source type: peer-reviewed primary genomic research
- Published/updated: 2021-07-16
- Accessed: 2026-09-15
- Support notes: Study resequenced 110 accessions spanning feral plants, landraces, historical cultivars, hemp and drug types. Authors infer early Neolithic domestication in East Asia.
- Limits/counterpoint: This is an inference from modern genomes, not a directly dated ancient domesticated genome. Sampling of truly wild populations remains incomplete.
- Story use: Establish the strongest genomic case for East Asia while immediately qualifying certainty.
- Visual potential: map of East Asia; genome tree; landrace vs. modern cultivar.

### C02 — ~12,000 years is a model-based estimate, not a directly excavated date
- Status: SUPPORTED
- Claim: Ren et al. model an early domestication-related divergence around the start of the Holocene, approximately 12,000 years before present.
- Source: Ren et al. 2021, Science Advances.
- URL/identifier: https://doi.org/10.1126/sciadv.abg2286
- Source type: peer-reviewed primary genomic research
- Published/updated: 2021-07-16
- Accessed: 2026-09-15
- Support notes: The paper links its demographic estimate with early Neolithic archaeology and describes early domestication by that time.
- Limits/counterpoint: Dal Martello et al. 2024 caution that wild sampling is limited, model error bars are broad, and no ancient Cannabis genomic time series currently calibrates the estimate.
- Story use: Use the number as a model result with explicit qualification.
- Visual potential: timeline with a dotted/uncertain marker rather than a hard date.

### C03 — Archaeobotanists caution against treating genomic dates as exact
- Status: ESTABLISHED
- Claim: Archaeobotanical review work warns that the ~12,000 BP domestication estimate requires ground-truthing because wild populations are undersampled, error bars are wide, and ancient genomic calibration is lacking.
- Source: Dal Martello, R. et al., “Morphometric approaches to Cannabis evolution and differentiation from archaeological sites: interpreting the archaeobotanical evidence from bronze age Haimenkou, Yunnan,” Vegetation History and Archaeobotany 33, 503–518.
- URL/identifier: https://doi.org/10.1007/s00334-023-00966-6
- Source type: peer-reviewed archaeobotanical original/review research
- Published/updated: 2023-11-30 online; 2024 volume
- Accessed: 2026-09-15
- Support notes: Authors explicitly state that limited wild sampling and wide error bars call for empirical archaeobotanical ground-truthing and note the absence of ancient genomic data to calibrate timescales.
- Limits/counterpoint: This is a methodological caution, not evidence that the genomic model is wrong.
- Story use: Prevent false precision and create the documentary reframe: “use,” “cultivation,” and “domestication” are different claims.
- Visual potential: side-by-side genome model and charred archaeological seed.

### C04 — Early Cannabis was probably multipurpose before stronger specialization
- Status: SUPPORTED
- Claim: The 2021 genomic study proposes that early domesticated Cannabis functioned primarily as a multipurpose crop before stronger divergent selection for fiber and drug production around ~4,000 BP.
- Source: Ren et al. 2021, Science Advances.
- URL/identifier: https://doi.org/10.1126/sciadv.abg2286
- Source type: peer-reviewed primary genomic research
- Published/updated: 2021-07-16
- Accessed: 2026-09-15
- Support notes: Authors explicitly propose early multipurpose use followed by strong divergent selection roughly 4,000 BP.
- Limits/counterpoint: “Multipurpose” is a reconstruction combining genetic and archaeological context, not direct observation of a single early field.
- Story use: Core thesis. Avoid “hemp first” or “drug first.”
- Visual potential: same plant splitting into seed, stem fiber, and flower/resin uses.

### C05 — Hemp/drug specialization is estimated at roughly 4,000 years ago
- Status: SUPPORTED
- Claim: Genomic demographic analyses place expansion/divergence of specialized hemp and drug types around 4,000 years BP.
- Source: Ren et al. 2021, Science Advances; discussed critically by Dal Martello et al. 2024.
- URL/identifier: https://doi.org/10.1126/sciadv.abg2286 ; https://doi.org/10.1007/s00334-023-00966-6
- Source type: primary genomics + archaeobotanical synthesis
- Published/updated: 2021; 2024
- Accessed: 2026-09-15
- Support notes: Ren et al. state that a small number of early domesticated strains expanded to form hemp and drug types ~4,000 BP. Dal Martello et al. summarize divergence around ca. 3,800 BP.
- Limits/counterpoint: Treat as approximate and model-dependent.
- Story use: Mid-video branching point.
- Visual potential: timeline branch around Bronze Age.

### C06 — Fiber and drug selection favored contrasting plant architecture
- Status: SUPPORTED
- Claim: Genomic selection signals are consistent with tall, relatively unbranched hemp optimized for bast fiber versus shorter, more-branched drug-type plants maximizing flowers and resin.
- Source: Ren et al. 2021, Science Advances.
- URL/identifier: https://doi.org/10.1126/sciadv.abg2286
- Source type: peer-reviewed primary genomic research
- Published/updated: 2021-07-16
- Accessed: 2026-09-15
- Support notes: Study reports selection signals in genes related to branching, flowering, lignin and cellulose biology, and interprets them in relation to contrasting crop architecture.
- Limits/counterpoint: Candidate-gene and sweep signals support a selection history but do not mean every modern hemp or drug plant fits a simple morphology.
- Story use: Explain how human preference becomes visible anatomy.
- Visual potential: split-screen tall hemp field vs. branched flowering drug plant.

### C07 — Cannabinoid synthase genes show selection-linked loss-of-function patterns
- Status: SUPPORTED
- Claim: In the 2021 dataset, most drug-type samples retained complete THCAS while showing CBDAS pseudogenization, whereas hemp types more often retained CBDAS and lost complete THCAS; authors infer ancestral functional possession of both systems followed by selection-linked loss of function.
- Source: Ren et al. 2021, Science Advances.
- URL/identifier: https://doi.org/10.1126/sciadv.abg2286
- Source type: peer-reviewed primary genomic research
- Published/updated: 2021-07-16
- Accessed: 2026-09-15
- Support notes: Paper explicitly discusses extensive loss of full THCAS in hemp-type and CBDAS in drug-type cultivars under strong selection.
- Limits/counterpoint: Cannabinoid inheritance is structurally complex; gene copy number, pseudogenes, hybridization and regulatory variation complicate a simple two-gene story.
- Story use: Show that humans selected chemistry as well as shape.
- Visual potential: simplified enzyme fork from shared precursor to THC/CBD pathways.

### C08 — Cannabis populations have a mosaic hybridization history and Asian diversity is undersampled
- Status: ESTABLISHED
- Claim: A 2025 cannabis pangenome found extensive structural/genetic variation, evidence of repeated gene flow among hemp and drug lineages, underrepresentation of Asian germplasm, and a possibility that wild relatives persist in Asia.
- Source: “Domesticated cannabinoid synthases amid a wild mosaic cannabis pangenome,” Nature.
- URL/identifier: https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-025-09065-0
- Source type: peer-reviewed primary pangenomic research
- Published/updated: 2025
- Accessed: 2026-09-15
- Support notes: Study constructed a pangenome from 193 genomes (181 new plus 12 previously released) representing 144 biological samples; reports hybridization and emphasizes unresolved wild diversity.
- Limits/counterpoint: Current pangenome still does not sample all Asian wild/feral diversity.
- Story use: Modern reframe: the domestication history is braided, not a clean tree.
- Visual potential: network rather than tree; arrows of gene flow across Eurasia.

### C09 — Cannabis is usually dioecious and human management of sex can change selection pressure
- Status: SUPPORTED
- Claim: Cannabis is usually dioecious, with male and female flowers on separate plants; reproductive management differs by crop goal, and reducing male pollen is useful for production of unpollinated drug-type female flowers, while seed and fiber systems have different incentives.
- Source: Dal Martello et al. 2024; Nature 2025 pangenome discussion.
- URL/identifier: https://doi.org/10.1007/s00334-023-00966-6 ; https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-025-09065-0
- Source type: peer-reviewed archaeobotanical synthesis + primary genomics
- Published/updated: 2024; 2025
- Accessed: 2026-09-15
- Support notes: Dal Martello et al. discuss separate sexes, cross-pollination, and different management incentives; Nature 2025 notes that pollination reduces cannabinoid yield in female plants and males are reduced/eliminated in drug breeding.
- Limits/counterpoint: Ancient field-management practices cannot be assumed to match modern cultivation exactly.
- Story use: Explain the reproductive mechanism through which preferences become inherited.
- Visual potential: male pollen drifting vs. isolated flowering females; dense fiber field.

### C10 — Haimenkou preserves an intermediate/overlapping domestication signal
- Status: SUPPORTED
- Claim: More than 800 Cannabis grains were recovered at Haimenkou, Yunnan, from contexts between 1650 and 400 BCE; most measured seeds fall in overlapping modern psychoactive/fiber ranges, which researchers interpret as evidence of a crop diversifying toward specialized uses.
- Source: Dal Martello et al. 2024, Vegetation History and Archaeobotany.
- URL/identifier: https://doi.org/10.1007/s00334-023-00966-6
- Source type: peer-reviewed archaeobotanical research
- Published/updated: 2023-11-30 online; 2024 volume
- Accessed: 2026-09-15
- Support notes: Over 800 grains recovered; ~700 from one context dated 1400–1100 BCE. Morphometrics mostly overlap psychoactive/fiber ranges, with some larger fiber/oil-type values.
- Limits/counterpoint: Seed size ranges overlap; authors explicitly present the inference cautiously.
- Story use: Concrete archaeological example of specialization in progress.
- Visual potential: macro shots/SEM of charred seeds; scatterplot of seed sizes.

### C11 — Human use of Cannabis was geographically broad and may be multiregional even if domestication had a major center
- Status: SUPPORTED
- Claim: A systematic review of archaeological and palaeoenvironmental records proposed a multiregional origin of human use and identified a marked rise in Cannabis achene records in East Asia around 5,000–4,000 cal BP, connected to expanding Eurasian exchange.
- Source: Long, T., Wagner, M., Demske, D., Leipe, C., Tarasov, P., “Cannabis in Eurasia: origin of human use and Bronze Age trans-continental connections,” Vegetation History and Archaeobotany 26.
- URL/identifier: https://doi.org/10.1007/s00334-016-0579-6
- Source type: peer-reviewed systematic archaeological/palaeoenvironmental review
- Published/updated: 2017
- Accessed: 2026-09-15
- Support notes: Review considers fibers, pollen, achenes and impressions and proposes multiregional human use; achene evidence rises strongly in East Asia around 5,000–4,000 BP.
- Limits/counterpoint: “Multiregional human use” is not equivalent to multiple independent domestications.
- Story use: Distinguish center of domestication from later use/dispersal.
- Visual potential: Eurasian map with multiple early-use points and steppe exchange routes.

### C12 — Jirzankal provides early chemically verified evidence of psychoactive Cannabis burning
- Status: ESTABLISHED
- Claim: At Jirzankal Cemetery in the eastern Pamirs, around 500 BCE, GC-MS analysis of wooden braziers and burned stones detected cannabinoid residues consistent with deliberate burning of Cannabis with relatively high psychoactive potential during mortuary ceremonies.
- Source: Ren, M. et al., “The origins of cannabis smoking: Chemical residue evidence from the first millennium BCE in the Pamirs,” Science Advances 5(6), eaaw1391.
- URL/identifier: https://doi.org/10.1126/sciadv.aaw1391 ; PMID 31206023 ; PMCID PMC6561734
- Source type: peer-reviewed primary archaeological chemistry
- Published/updated: 2019-06-12
- Accessed: 2026-09-15
- Support notes: CBN, an oxidative metabolite of THC, was found on internal charred surfaces of most tested braziers and on some stones; exterior controls lacked cannabinoids. Authors describe it as some of the earliest directly dated and scientifically verified evidence for ritual cannabis smoking.
- Limits/counterpoint: The evidence does not establish that psychoactive use caused initial domestication, nor whether high-THC plants were cultivated, selected, hybridized, or locally high-producing wild/feral populations.
- Story use: Cinematic opening/payoff and chronological contrast with much earlier domestication estimates.
- Visual potential: Jirzankal brazier, hot stones, tomb landscape, GC-MS chromatogram.

### C13 — Cannabis archaeological evidence requires source-specific caution
- Status: ESTABLISHED
- Claim: Pollen can indicate local Cannabis/Humulus presence but does not by itself prove cultivation or human use; early textile identifications from impressions can also be uncertain. Seeds and securely identified fibers generally provide stronger evidence.
- Source: Dal Martello et al. 2024.
- URL/identifier: https://doi.org/10.1007/s00334-023-00966-6
- Source type: peer-reviewed archaeobotanical synthesis
- Published/updated: 2024
- Accessed: 2026-09-15
- Support notes: Authors discuss Cannabis/Humulus pollen similarity, inability of pollen to distinguish wild from cultivated forms, and problems with some fiber/impression claims.
- Limits/counterpoint: Context matters; no single material class is perfect.
- Story use: Teach viewer why “first Cannabis” headlines can oversimplify evidence.
- Visual potential: pollen grain, seed, textile impression with evidence-strength labels.

## Unverified / blocked claims

- Exact geographic point (“the first village”) where Cannabis was domesticated: UNVERIFIED / currently unknowable from available evidence.
- Exact calendar date of domestication: UNVERIFIED; ~12,000 BP is a demographic model estimate.
- Claim that Cannabis was originally domesticated specifically for intoxication: UNSUPPORTED and excluded.
- Claim that Cannabis was originally domesticated specifically for fiber: too strong; early multipurpose use is better supported.
- Claim that Jirzankal is the single “oldest” psychoactive Cannabis use anywhere: avoid; source says “some of the earliest” directly dated and verified evidence.
- Claim that modern hemp and marijuana are separate species: taxonomy is contested; avoid in narration.
- Claim that every modern drug plant lost CBDAS or every hemp plant lost THCAS: too absolute; use “most samples in the 2021 dataset” and explain complexity.

## Sources rejected and why

- Wikipedia / general web summaries: useful only for orientation; not used as support for central claims.
- News headlines claiming “Cannabis was domesticated exactly 12,000 years ago in China”: oversimplify a genomic estimate and do not preserve uncertainty.
- Unsourced historical claims about ancient shamans or the “first marijuana smokers”: excluded unless tied to primary archaeological evidence.
- Commercial cannabis sites and seed-company histories: excluded for central factual claims.
