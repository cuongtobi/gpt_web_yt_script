# Research Ledger

## Provisional central question

How did repeated human selection transform wild Cannabis into materially different cultivated forms, and what can the evidence actually tell us about where, when, and why that process began?

## Causal steps to prove

1. Establish the plausible pre-domestication relationship between people and wild Cannabis without assuming a single purpose.
2. Show which plant traits humans could have favored for fiber, seed, architecture, flowering behavior, or chemical profile.
3. Establish whether archaeological evidence demonstrates cultivation, use, processing, or only plant presence—and keep those categories separate.
4. Establish what genomic evidence can and cannot infer about population structure, divergence, and domestication history.
5. Explain how different use-cases could create divergent selection pressures that eventually produce hemp-like and drug-type cultivated forms.
6. Identify major geographic/time turning points only where primary or peer-reviewed evidence supports them.
7. Connect ancient selection to the modern diversity of Cannabis without implying a simple linear path if the evidence indicates admixture, feralization, or multiple selection episodes.

## Unverified / blocked claims

- Any exact domestication date or date range.
- Any claim naming a single geographic “center” as settled fact.
- Any “first cultivated,” “oldest Cannabis,” or “first psychoactive use” statement.
- Any specific archaeological site, object, residue, pollen record, seed assemblage, or textile date.
- Any exact genomic divergence estimate, sample count, clade label, or inferred migration route.
- Any named gene/allele claimed to explain fiber or cannabinoid traits.
- Any consensus wording such as “scientists agree” without a synthesis source.
