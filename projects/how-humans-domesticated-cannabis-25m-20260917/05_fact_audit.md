## Selected hook audit

Selected hook: H4 — Reverse assumption.

The premise — that cannabis was probably not domesticated primarily to create a stronger intoxicant, and that early domesticated cannabis was likely multipurpose — is supported by the 2021 whole-genome reconstruction. The paper proposes that early domesticated Cannabis was used primarily as a multipurpose crop until roughly 4,000 years BP, before strong divergent selection toward fiber and drug production. The hook correctly keeps the qualifier “probably” and does not present this interpretation as a directly observed Neolithic motive.

Action: KEEP.

Primary source: Ren et al., “Large-scale whole-genome resequencing unravels the domestication history of Cannabis sativa,” Science Advances 7(29), 2021, eabg2286, doi:10.1126/sciadv.abg2286.

## Required changes

No blocker requires a hook change or Story Spine redesign. Final edit must preserve the following qualifications already present in the draft:

- Treat ~12,000 years BP as a genomic demographic-model estimate with a broad interval, not a single archaeological domestication date.
- Attribute East Asian domestication to the 2021 genomic reconstruction; do not present the deeper natural geographic origin of Cannabis as settled.
- Keep “wild” versus “feral” unresolved; do not state that every wild-growing cannabis population is an escaped crop.
- Treat ~4,000 years BP as the modeled period when hemp- and drug-associated trajectories strongly diverged/intensified, not a universal one-time split.
- Treat Haimenkou seed morphometrics as evidence consistent with diversification, not as a perfect classifier of fiber, oilseed, or psychoactive end use.
- Treat Jirzankal as evidence of psychoactive cannabis burning around 500 BCE and among the earliest directly dated/scientifically verified examples; do not call it the first psychoactive cannabis use in human history.
- Do not claim Jirzankal proves a standardized deliberately bred high-THC cultivar. Preserve deliberate selection, naturally potent populations, hybridization, and environmental/plastic responses as alternatives.
