# 02 — Story Architecture

## Viewer promise
Người xem sẽ rời video với một mô hình chính xác hơn: congenital total blindness không phải “một màn hình đen”, và thiếu thị giác không ngăn não xây không gian, khái niệm hay tái phân công vùng chẩm cho thông tin không thị giác.

## Central contradiction
Một người có thể chưa từng có hình ảnh thị giác, nhưng vẫn có thể biết hình dạng, không gian, màu sắc ở mức khái niệm — và cả vùng não gọi là “visual cortex” vẫn hoạt động mạnh cho sound/touch/language.

## Hook archetype
Defamiliarization + Object-First Contradiction. Dùng chính “màn hình đen” như một object quen thuộc rồi chỉ ra rằng black itself is visual, từ đó buộc viewer sửa câu hỏi.

## Central question
Nếu một người mù hoàn toàn từ khi sinh chưa từng có visual experience, họ thực sự có loại trải nghiệm và world-model nào, và não bộ xử lý không gian/khái niệm/thông tin không thị giác ra sao?

## Act Architecture

### ACT 1 — Black is not nothing
- Viewer belief/question entering: “Blindness = seeing black.”
- Payoff: black is itself visual; clinical blindness is heterogeneous; focus total congenital blindness.
- Scale/depth increase: object (black screen) → individual phenomenology.
- Act-ending consequence: nếu không phải black image, thế giới nội tâm được tổ chức bằng gì?

### ACT 2 — A world without pictures can still have structure
- Viewer belief/question entering: không có pictures có đồng nghĩa không có shape/space/color concepts?
- Payoff: spatial representations and conceptual color knowledge can exist without visual experience.
- Scale/depth increase: individual tasks → language/community knowledge.
- Act-ending consequence: nếu cognition vẫn rich, brain areas “for vision” đang làm gì?

### ACT 3 — The “visual” brain changes jobs
- Viewer belief/question entering: visual cortex idle?
- Payoff: occipital cortex participates in sound/touch/language; activation ≠ visual qualia.
- Scale/depth increase: individual behavior → neural system.
- Act-ending consequence: compensation is not universal super-sense; development involves recalibration.

### ACT 4 — First sight exposes what had to be learned
- Viewer belief/question entering: if a nonvisual world-model exists, does new vision plug into it instantly?
- Payoff: Project Prakash cross-modal mapping is initially weak then improves rapidly.
- Scale/depth increase: system mechanism → developmental learning over time.
- Act-ending consequence: seeing is not just input arriving; mapping/interpreting input is learned.

### ACT 5 — Replace the wrong question
- Viewer belief/question entering: so what do they “see”?
- Payoff: for total congenital blindness, safest answer is no ordinary visual content rather than a black field; rich nonvisual world-model can coexist with no visual qualia.
- Scale/depth increase: individual phenomenology → general principle of brain organization.
- Ending consequence: the black screen was a sighted-person metaphor, not a universal report.

## Viewer-question chain

Q1: Is congenital blindness just endless black?
- Trigger: black-screen object.
- Payoff: black is a visual state; total congenital blindness is better described as no visual experience/content, with important residual-vision exceptions.
- Consequence: then how is space represented?

Q2: Can someone have spatial imagery without visual pictures?
- Trigger: absence of visual content.
- Payoff: yes, many spatial/pictorial tasks can be done using nonvisual representations.
- Consequence: if shape/space survive, what about a uniquely visual property like color?

Q3: Can someone understand color without ever seeing it?
- Trigger: nonvisual spatial representation.
- Payoff: rich causal/conceptual color knowledge can be learned linguistically, though qualia and some associations differ.
- Consequence: knowledge and sensory experience are separable; dream reports become a test case.

Q4: Do dreams reveal hidden visual imagery?
- Trigger: concept vs qualia distinction.
- Payoff: dream evidence is mixed/debated; nonvisual senses dominate many reports.
- Consequence: subjective reports alone cannot settle mechanism; look at brain organization.

Q5: What happens to visual cortex without vision?
- Trigger: phenomenology uncertainty.
- Payoff: cross-modal recruitment for touch/sound/language.
- Consequence: does that mean superior senses or “seeing sound”?

Q6: Does blindness automatically sharpen other senses?
- Trigger: occipital recruitment.
- Payoff: no; advantages and deficits are task-specific.
- Consequence: vision may calibrate multisensory maps, so restored sight is a revealing experiment.

Q7: If sight arrives later, does the brain instantly know what it is seeing?
- Trigger: recalibration idea.
- Payoff: touch→vision mapping can initially fail and then improve rapidly.
- Consequence: “seeing” itself contains learned mappings.

Q8: So what is the best final answer?
- Trigger: first-sight evidence.
- Payoff: no universal screen capture; total congenital blindness can mean absence of ordinary visual content alongside rich nonvisual cognition.
- Consequence: reframe brain as information/world-model builder rather than camera.

## Causal ladder
1. Congenital visual deprivation removes/reduces visual input during development (C01, C02).
2. Therefore “black screen” is not a neutral description; it imports a visual quality (C03).
3. The brain still receives touch, sound, proprioception and language, allowing spatial/semantic representations to develop (C04, C05).
4. Repeated nonvisual information + developmental plasticity recruits occipital networks for nonvisual tasks (C07, C08, C10).
5. This reorganization can improve some tasks but cannot be summarized as universal sensory enhancement (C09).
6. Because cross-sensory mappings are learned/calibrated, newly available vision does not instantly translate tactile models into visual recognition (C11).
7. Rapid post-surgery learning shows a flexible but experience-dependent mapping process (C11, C12).
8. Therefore the title question is answered at the level of modality/representation, not by assigning a single color to consciousness (C03, C13).
