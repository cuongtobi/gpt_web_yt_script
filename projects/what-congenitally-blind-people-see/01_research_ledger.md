# 01 — Research Ledger

## Provisional central question
Nếu một người mù hoàn toàn từ khi sinh ra chưa từng có trải nghiệm thị giác, “họ nhìn thấy gì” thực sự có nghĩa gì, và bộ não của họ xây thế giới, không gian, khái niệm và chức năng vùng chẩm ra sao khi thiếu thị giác từ đầu?

## Causal steps cần chứng minh
1. “Blindness” là một phổ; phải tách total congenital blindness khỏi residual vision.
2. “Thấy màu đen” vẫn là một mô tả thị giác; với total congenital blindness, khoa học nên nói về absence of visual experience hơn là gán một màu chủ quan.
3. Không có thị giác không loại bỏ biểu diễn không gian/hình dạng.
4. Kiến thức về màu và appearance có thể được học qua ngôn ngữ dù không có color qualia.
5. Dream imagery ở congenital blindness phức tạp và còn tranh luận; không được dùng như bằng chứng tuyệt đối.
6. Vùng chẩm có thể được tuyển dụng cho touch/sound/language trong early/congenital blindness.
7. Cross-modal plasticity không đồng nghĩa mọi giác quan đều “siêu phàm”; lợi thế phụ thuộc task và có cả deficit ở một số task không gian.
8. Khi thị giác được phục hồi sau congenital/early blindness, mapping touch→vision không xuất hiện hoàn chỉnh ngay lập tức nhưng có thể học rất nhanh.
9. Central answer phải tách visual qualia khỏi spatial/semantic world model.

### C01 — Blindness is a spectrum, not a single perceptual state
- Status: ESTABLISHED
- Claim: Vision impairment/blindness bao gồm nhiều mức suy giảm; nhiều người còn một phần thị lực chức năng. Vì vậy không thể suy từ nhãn “mù” ra một trải nghiệm thị giác duy nhất.
- Source: World Health Organization — Blindness and vision impairment; WHO Europe — Eye and vision care Q&A
- URL/identifier: https://www.who.int/en/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/blindness-and-visual-impairment ; https://www.who.int/europe/news-room/questions-and-answers/item/eye-and-vision-care
- Source type: official institutional synthesis
- Published/updated: 2026-02-10; 2023-06-07
- Accessed: 2026-09-15
- Support notes: WHO mô tả vision impairment là suy giảm chức năng thị giác và nêu rằng nhiều người có partial inability to see.
- Limits/counterpoint: WHO classification không trực tiếp mô tả qualia của congenital total blindness.
- Story role: setup
- Story value: HIGH
- Visual potential: MEDIUM
- Playable scene / concrete anchor: black screen vs residual light/contrast examples as generic graphic, not a claim about one person.
- Human-scale example: một người có thể chỉ nhận biết ánh sáng trong khi người khác không có usable visual input.
- Scale potential: individual
- Escalation value: changes interpretation

### C02 — Congenitally blind participants can truly lack color/shape/motion experience
- Status: ESTABLISHED
- Claim: Trong nghiên cứu PNAS về kiến thức màu, nhóm congenitally blind báo cáo không có trải nghiệm về color, shape hoặc motion và nhiều nhất chỉ có minimal light perception.
- Source: Kim et al., “Shared understanding of color among sighted and blind adults”
- URL/identifier: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8379969/
- Source type: peer-reviewed primary study (PNAS)
- Published/updated: 2021
- Accessed: 2026-09-15
- Support notes: 20 congenitally blind participants; methods explicitly describe no experience with color, shape, motion and at most minimal light perception.
- Limits/counterpoint: self-report + selected sample; không đại diện mọi cause of congenital blindness.
- Story role: setup
- Story value: HIGH
- Visual potential: LOW
- Playable scene / concrete anchor: research participant answering color/appearance tasks; no invented behavior beyond task description.
- Human-scale example: người chưa từng có color experience vẫn tham gia reasoning tasks về màu.
- Scale potential: individual
- Escalation value: changes interpretation

### C03 — “Seeing black” should be treated as an interpretation, not a measured universal fact
- Status: INTERPRETATION
- Claim: Với người total congenitally blind chưa có visual experience, gán “một trường nhìn màu đen” nhập một visual quality vào trạng thái chưa được chứng minh có visual qualia; wording an toàn hơn là absence of visual experience/content, đồng thời thừa nhận science không trực tiếp đọc được qualia.
- Source: C02 + broader behavioral/neuroscience literature; epistemic inference
- URL/identifier: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8379969/
- Source type: interpretation grounded in participant criteria and limits of subjective measurement
- Published/updated: 2021
- Accessed: 2026-09-15
- Support notes: sample explicitly had no color/shape/motion experience; black itself is a visual quality, so “black screen” is not a neutral synonym for no vision.
- Limits/counterpoint: Không thể kiểm chứng trực tiếp phenomenology của mọi congenital blindness; wording phải tránh “100% không thấy đen” như một measured fact.
- Story role: payoff
- Story value: HIGH
- Visual potential: HIGH
- Playable scene / concrete anchor: black screen as an object/concept, then remove it as an inadequate analogy.
- Human-scale example: contrast between a sighted viewer’s black screen and no prior visual experience.
- Scale potential: individual
- Escalation value: changes interpretation

### C04 — Spatial/pictorial tasks can be performed without visual experience
- Status: SUPPORTED
- Claim: Congenitally totally blind participants can perform tasks often mediated by visual mental imagery in sighted people, including spatial imagery and shape comparison, although performance and representational style can differ.
- Source: Aleman et al., “Visual imagery without visual experience: evidence from congenitally totally blind people”
- URL/identifier: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11496156/
- Source type: peer-reviewed experimental study
- Published/updated: 2001-08-08
- Accessed: 2026-09-15
- Support notes: blind participants performed spatial and pictorial imagery tasks; authors suggest shared representations across vision and haptics.
- Limits/counterpoint: “visual imagery task” không chứng minh subjective visual pictures; sample size/study age limit generalization.
- Story role: mechanism
- Story value: HIGH
- Visual potential: HIGH
- Playable scene / concrete anchor: 2D/3D cube positions and shape comparison task.
- Human-scale example: giữ vị trí một cube trong ma trận 2D/3D bằng mental operations.
- Scale potential: individual
- Escalation value: deepens mechanism

### C05 — Congenitally blind people can acquire rich conceptual knowledge of color
- Status: ESTABLISHED
- Claim: Sighted and congenitally blind adults can share causal/inferential understanding of color and object-color regularities, dù blind participants ít đồng nhất hơn ở một số canonical color facts.
- Source: Kim et al., “Shared understanding of color among sighted and blind adults”
- URL/identifier: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8379969/
- Source type: peer-reviewed primary study (PNAS)
- Published/updated: 2021
- Accessed: 2026-09-15
- Support notes: blind/sighted groups made similar inferences about color consistency and explanations; differences remained for specific color associations.
- Limits/counterpoint: conceptual knowledge ≠ color qualia.
- Story role: reframe
- Story value: HIGH
- Visual potential: HIGH
- Playable scene / concrete anchor: banana/stop-sign/car color reasoning task.
- Human-scale example: biết banana thường “yellow” và hiểu color can be function-relevant without seeing yellow.
- Scale potential: individual → community
- Escalation value: changes interpretation

### C06 — Dream sensory content in congenital blindness is nonvisual-heavy, but visual-like reports are debated
- Status: DEBATED
- Claim: Reports of congenitally blind dreams often contain more auditory, haptic, olfactory and gustatory content; whether they contain genuinely visual imagery remains disputed, with some analyses reporting visual-like content.
- Source: “Mental Imagery in Dreams of Congenitally Blind People”
- URL/identifier: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10605848/
- Source type: peer-reviewed analysis/review using DreamBank reports
- Published/updated: 2023
- Accessed: 2026-09-15
- Support notes: analysis of 180 dreams from seven congenitally blind people found elevated nonvisual sensory content and also noted visual imagery reports.
- Limits/counterpoint: very small participant count; retrospective/report coding; cannot settle qualia.
- Story role: reframe
- Story value: MEDIUM
- Visual potential: MEDIUM
- Playable scene / concrete anchor: dream-report text/diagram; no invented dream scene.
- Human-scale example: sensory dream report dominated by sound/touch/smell rather than assuming cinema-like imagery.
- Scale potential: individual
- Escalation value: changes interpretation

### C07 — Early blindness produces cross-modal recruitment of occipital cortex
- Status: ESTABLISHED
- Claim: Early-blind participants show greater occipital responses to auditory and tactile tasks than sighted controls, demonstrating cross-modal plasticity.
- Source: Fine et al., “Mechanisms of Cross-Modal Plasticity in Early-Blind Subjects”
- URL/identifier: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3007643/
- Source type: peer-reviewed neuroimaging study
- Published/updated: 2010
- Accessed: 2026-09-15
- Support notes: enhanced BOLD responses in occipital cortex across auditory/tactile tasks in early blind participants.
- Limits/counterpoint: BOLD activation does not reveal subjective visual qualia and does not mean sound literally becomes vision.
- Story role: escalation
- Story value: HIGH
- Visual potential: HIGH
- Playable scene / concrete anchor: fMRI brain maps while listening/touching.
- Human-scale example: sound/touch task recruits posterior cortex normally associated with vision.
- Scale potential: individual → system
- Escalation value: deepens mechanism

### C08 — Occipital cortex can participate in spoken-language processing in congenital blindness
- Status: ESTABLISHED
- Claim: In congenitally blind adults and blind children, occipital cortex can respond to spoken language; childhood data show language responses can emerge early in development.
- Source: Bedny et al., “Language processing in the occipital cortex of congenitally blind adults”; Lane et al., “Visual Cortex Responds to Spoken Language in Blind Children”
- URL/identifier: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3060248/ ; https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6605232/
- Source type: peer-reviewed fMRI studies
- Published/updated: 2011; 2015
- Accessed: 2026-09-15
- Support notes: occipital regions recruited for language; child study reports spoken-language responses present by age 4 in sampled blind children.
- Limits/counterpoint: group-level neural activation; exact functional contribution and developmental pathway remain active research topics.
- Story role: reversal
- Story value: HIGH
- Visual potential: HIGH
- Playable scene / concrete anchor: child/adult hears spoken sentences inside scanner; activation map.
- Human-scale example: a “visual” cortical area responding while no visual stimulus is present.
- Scale potential: individual → system
- Escalation value: changes interpretation

### C09 — Blindness does not create universal super-senses
- Status: ESTABLISHED
- Claim: Some blind individuals outperform sighted people on selected auditory/tactile tasks, but congenital blindness can also impair certain auditory spatial computations; compensation is task-specific, not a universal boost.
- Source: “A Functional Neuroimaging Study of Sound Localization…”; “Impairment of auditory spatial localization in congenitally blind human subjects”
- URL/identifier: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC544927/ ; https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3891446/
- Source type: peer-reviewed behavioral/neuroimaging studies
- Published/updated: 2005; 2013
- Accessed: 2026-09-15
- Support notes: superior monaural localization in a subgroup correlated with occipital activation; separate study found marked impairment in auditory spatial bisection despite preserved simpler tasks.
- Limits/counterpoint: task/sample differences mean results should not be generalized to every blind individual.
- Story role: reversal
- Story value: HIGH
- Visual potential: HIGH
- Playable scene / concrete anchor: multiple speakers around participant; simple localization vs spatial-bisection task.
- Human-scale example: better at one sound task, worse at another.
- Scale potential: individual
- Escalation value: changes interpretation

### C10 — Expert blind echolocators can recruit navigation-related occipital cortex
- Status: SUPPORTED
- Claim: In a small 2023 fMRI study, blind expert echolocators recruited the occipital place area for coherent echo-acoustic route information, unlike control groups.
- Source: “The Occipital Place Area Is Recruited for Echo-Acoustically Guided Navigation in Blind Human Echolocators”
- URL/identifier: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10278676/
- Source type: peer-reviewed fMRI study
- Published/updated: 2023
- Accessed: 2026-09-15
- Support notes: 6 expert blind echolocators; coherent route sounds produced greater OPA activity.
- Limits/counterpoint: tiny expert sample; cannot generalize echolocation expertise to blind population.
- Story role: escalation
- Story value: MEDIUM
- Visual potential: HIGH
- Playable scene / concrete anchor: clicks/echo route through maze + fMRI map.
- Human-scale example: expert uses reflected sound to infer route geometry.
- Scale potential: individual → system
- Escalation value: deepens mechanism

### C11 — Newly sighted people may not immediately map touch to vision
- Status: ESTABLISHED
- Claim: Project Prakash studies found that shortly after sight-restoring surgery, children/young people with congenital or early-onset treatable blindness could not reliably recognize by sight objects known by touch; cross-modal matching improved rapidly, in some cases within about a week.
- Source: Project Prakash review + Molyneux-task summary
- URL/identifier: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3866088/ ; https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3238323/
- Source type: peer-reviewed review/clinical research summary of primary experiments
- Published/updated: 2011–2013
- Accessed: 2026-09-15
- Support notes: immediate cross-modal transfer poor; substantial improvement after short experience.
- Limits/counterpoint: rare treatable conditions, small cohorts, variable histories; not equivalent to every form of total congenital blindness.
- Story role: reversal
- Story value: HIGH
- Visual potential: HIGH
- Playable scene / concrete anchor: hand explores unfamiliar 3D object; after surgery eyes inspect matching objects.
- Human-scale example: touch-to-touch and vision-to-vision can work while touch-to-vision initially fails.
- Scale potential: individual → system
- Escalation value: changes interpretation

### C12 — Vision onset can rapidly alter spatial imagery skills
- Status: SUPPORTED
- Claim: Children who gained sight after years of congenital/early blindness showed improvement in spatial imagery after sight onset, supporting a contribution of visual experience to some spatial imagery abilities.
- Source: “Improvement in spatial imagery following sight onset late in childhood”
- URL/identifier: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4456678/
- Source type: peer-reviewed longitudinal study
- Published/updated: 2014
- Accessed: 2026-09-15
- Support notes: improvements observed after sight-restoring surgery.
- Limits/counterpoint: small clinical sample; does not imply pre-surgery spatial representation was absent.
- Story role: escalation
- Story value: MEDIUM
- Visual potential: MEDIUM
- Playable scene / concrete anchor: same spatial task before and after sight onset.
- Human-scale example: skill changes when a new sensory channel becomes available.
- Scale potential: individual
- Escalation value: deepens mechanism

### C13 — Occipital activation is not itself evidence of visual conscious experience
- Status: INTERPRETATION
- Claim: fMRI/PET evidence supports functional recruitment of occipital cortex, not the claim that blind participants experience sound/touch as visual qualia.
- Source: methodological boundary across C07–C10
- URL/identifier: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3007643/
- Source type: interpretation constrained by neuroimaging method
- Published/updated: 2010–2023 literature
- Accessed: 2026-09-15
- Support notes: cited papers report task-related neural activity; none can directly decode subjective qualia as a screen recording.
- Limits/counterpoint: subjective reports and other methods can add evidence, but cannot erase first-person uncertainty.
- Story role: callback
- Story value: HIGH
- Visual potential: MEDIUM
- Playable scene / concrete anchor: activation map beside warning label “activity ≠ qualia.”
- Human-scale example: same fMRI color map can indicate computation without specifying what it feels like.
- Scale potential: system
- Escalation value: changes interpretation
